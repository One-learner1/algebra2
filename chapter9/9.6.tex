\section*{Derivation of Heron's Formula}
First, let us note that $\sin^2\theta+\cos^2\theta=1$. For consider an angle $\theta$ intersecting the unit circle at $(x,y)$. Then we know that $\cos\theta=x$ and 
$\sin\theta=y$. But by the Pythagorean theorem, $x^2+y^2=1$; hence $\sin^2\theta+\cos^2\theta=1$. This also means that
\[ \textrm{(1) }\sin\theta=\sqrt{1-\cos^2\theta} \] Now consider a triangle with side lengths $a,b,c$. By the law of cosines, we know that
\[ c^2=a^2+b^2-2ab\cos C \]
\[ 2ab\cos C=a^2+b^2-c^2 \]
\[ \textrm{(2) }\cos C=\dfrac{a^2+b^2-c^2}{2ab} \]
Also, we know that the area of the triangle is $A=\frac{1}{2}ab\sin C$. Thus:
\[ A=\frac{1}{2}ab\sin C \]
\[ =\frac{1}{2}ab\sqrt{1-\cos^2 C}\textrm{ (By 1)} \]
\[ =\frac{1}{2}ab\sqrt{1-\left(\dfrac{a^2+b^2-c^2}{2ab}\right)^2}\textrm{ (By 2)} \]
\[ =\frac{1}{2}ab\sqrt{1-\dfrac{(a^2+b^2-c^2)^2}{4a^2b^2}} \]
\[ =\frac{1}{2}ab\sqrt{1-\dfrac{(a^2+b^2-c^2)a^2+(a^2+b^2-c^2)b^2-(a^2+b^2-c^2)c^2}{4a^2b^2}} \]
\[ =\frac{1}{2}ab\sqrt{1-\dfrac{a^4+a^2b^2-a^2c^2+a^2b^2+b^4-b^2c^2-a^2c^2-b^2c^2+c^4}{4a^2b^2}} \]
\[ =\frac{1}{2}ab\sqrt{1-\dfrac{a^4+b^4+c^4+2a^2b^2-2a^2c^2-2b^2c^2}{4a^2b^2}} \]
\[ =\frac{1}{2}ab\sqrt{\dfrac{4a^2b^2-(a^4+b^4+c^4+2a^2b^2-2a^2c^2-2b^2c^2)}{4a^2b^2}} \]
\[ =\frac{1}{2}ab\sqrt{\dfrac{2a^2b^2-a^4-b^4-c^4+2a^2c^2+2b^2c^2}{4a^2b^2}} \]
\[ =\frac{1}{2}ab\dfrac{\sqrt{2a^2b^2-a^4-b^4-c^4+2a^2c^2+2b^2c^2}}{2ab} \]
\[ \dfrac{\sqrt{2a^2b^2-a^4-b^4-c^4+2a^2c^2+2b^2c^2}}{4} \]
\[ \sqrt{\dfrac{2a^2b^2-a^4-b^4-c^4+2a^2c^2+2b^2c^2}{16}} \]
Now, one can see that
\[ \left(a+b+c\right)\left(-a+b+c\right)\left(a-b+c\right)\left(a+b-c\right)=2a^2b^2-a^4-b^4-c^4+2a^2c^2+2b^2c^2 \] meaning
\[ \sqrt{\dfrac{2a^2b^2-a^4-b^4-c^4+2a^2c^2+2b^2c^2}{16}} \]
\[ =\sqrt{\dfrac{\left(a+b+c\right)\left(-a+b+c\right)\left(a-b+c\right)\left(a+b-c\right)}{16}} \]
\[ =\sqrt{\left(\frac{a+b+c}{2}\right)\left(\frac{-a+b+c}{2}\right)\left(\frac{a-b+c}{2}\right)\left(\frac{a+b-c}{2}\right)} \]
Now recall that $s=\frac{1}{2}(a+b+c)$. Thus
\[ \sqrt{\left(\frac{a+b+c}{2}\right)\left(\frac{-a+b+c}{2}\right)\left(\frac{a-b+c}{2}\right)\left(\frac{a+b-c}{2}\right)} \]
\[ =\sqrt{s(s-a)(s-b)(s-c)} \] therefore proving Heron's Formula.

\section*{47}
(a): After 1 hour, the hiker walking north has gone 2 miles and the hiker walking east has gone 3 miles. Thus we have a triangle with sides of 2 miles and 3 miles and the angle between them 
$60^\circ$, where the third side is the distance $d$ between the two hikers after 1 hour. We can find this third side using the law of cosines:
\[ d=\sqrt{2^2+3^2-2(2)(3)\cos 60^\circ} \]
\[ d\approx 2.646 \] miles.


(b): We need to find what times $t$ are such that the distance between the two hikers is greater than $10$ miles. We know that the first hiker travels $2t$ miles in $t$ hours, while the 
second hiker travels $3t$ miles in $t$ hours. Thus the distance between the two hikers after $t$ hours is
\[ d=\sqrt{(2t)^2+(3t)^2-2(2t)(3t)\cos 60^\circ} \]
We need to solve the equation:
\[ \sqrt{(2t)^2+(3t)^2-2(2t)(3t)\cos 60^\circ}>10 \]
\[ (2t)^2+(3t)^2-2(2t)(3t)\cos 60^\circ>100 \]
\[ 4t^2+9t^2-12t^2\cos 60^\circ>100 \]
\[ 13t^2-6t^2>100\textrm{ (Because $\cos 60^\circ=\frac{1}{2}$)} \]
\[ 7t^2>100 \]
\[ t>\frac{10}{\sqrt{7}}=\frac{10\sqrt{7}}{7}\wedge t<-\frac{10}{\sqrt{7}}=-\frac{10\sqrt{7}}{7} \]
and because we are only interested in positive values of $t$, $t>\frac{10\sqrt{7}}{7}$. Thus the hikers become out of range after 
$\frac{10\sqrt{7}}{7}$ hours.


(c): After 2 hours, the first hiker will have gone 4 miles and the second hiker will have gone 6 miles. Thus the distance between the hikers is
\[ d=\sqrt{4^2+6^2-2(4)(6)\cos 60^\circ} \]
\[ d\approx 5.29 \]
miles. It will take the second hiker about $\frac{5.29}{3}\approx 1.763$ hours to reach the first hiker. Now, the direction the second hiker should walk is just the measure of the angle $\theta$ 
which can be found by the law of cosines:
\[ \cos\theta=\frac{6^2+5.29^2-4^2}{2(6)(5.29)} \]
\[ \theta\approx 40.897^\circ \]
Thus the hiker should walk northwest at an angle of about $40.897^\circ$.

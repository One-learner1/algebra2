\section*{16}
There are 13 hearts, meaning the total number of possible ways to choose 5 hearts is:
\[ \binom{13}{5}=\frac{13!}{(13-5)!\times 5!}=1287 \] This number is the same number as the possible ways to choose 5 diamonds (because there are the same number of hearts and diamonds), 
meaning the total number of possibilites to choose 5 hearts or 5 diamonds is $1287+1287=2574$.

\section*{17}
There are $\binom{4}{1}=4$ ways to choose exactly 1 queen. Then, taking the four queens out of the deck, there are then:
\[ \binom{48}{4}=194580 \] ways to choose the other four cards, meaning the total number of ways to choose exactly 1 queen in a five card deck is:
\[ 4\times 194580=778320 \] This handles the case where one of the five cards is a queen. On the other hand, consider the case where none of them are queens. Hence the total number of possibilities in this case is:
\[ \binom{48}{5}=1712304 \] meaning the total number of possibilites to choose at most 1 queen (zero or one queens) in a five card deck is:
\[ 778320+1712304=2490624 \]

\section*{37}
The pattern here is the same as the normal Pascal triangle formual:
\[ \binom{n}{0}+\binom{n}{1}+...+\binom{n}{n-1}+\binom{n}{n} \] except that as we move across the triangle, $n$ decreases by 1. Hence the pattern is:
\[ \binom{n}{0}+\binom{n-1}{1}+...+\binom{k}{j} \] and stop where $k=j$.

\section*{40}

four runners. After that, for every different choice of the four runners, there are $4!=24$ different ways to arrange them, meaning there are:

\section*{53}
(a): Consider some convex $n$-gon. Now, the connections between the vertices can be represented by finding the number of 2 objects within the $n$ 
vertices, and we can therefore use the formula:
\[ _nC_2 \]


(b): The only difference here is that the formula in part (a) also counts the actual sides of the polygon. Because we are considering an $n$-gon, we simply 
subtract $n$ from the number of line segments between the vertices, and hence the formula
\[ _nC_2-n \] for the number of diagonals.

\section*{1}
The inverse sine of $\frac{1}{2}$ is $\frac{\pi}{6}$, or $30^\circ$.

\section*{2}
$\tan\theta=\frac{opp}{adj}$ and $\cos\theta=\frac{adj}{hyp}$. Now, $hyp>adj$. But this means that 
$\frac{adj}{hyp}\ne 3$, and it is possible that $\frac{opp}{adj}=3$. Thus $\tan^{-1}3$ is defined, but 
$\cos^{-1}3$ is undefined.

\section*{30}
We suppose that $\cos\theta>0$ and $\sin\theta<0$. We can take $\theta$ to be $-30^\circ$, as
$\cos(-30^\circ)=\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}>0$ and $\sin(-30^\circ)=-\frac{1}{2}<0$.

\section*{31}
We suppose that $\sin\theta<0$ and $\tan\theta>0$. We can take $\theta$ to be $570^\circ$, which satisfies the required inequality for $\theta$. This is 
because $570^\circ$ is coterminal with $570-360=210^\circ$, and $210^\circ$ is in Quadrant III and has a reference angle of $210-180=30^\circ$, meaning
\[ \sin 210^\circ=-\sin 30^\circ=-\frac{1}{2}<0 \] and \[ \tan 210^\circ=\tan 30^\circ=\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}>0 \]
that is, $\sin 570^\circ<0$ and $\tan 570^\circ>0$.

\section*{37}
We know that the rock salt is stored 11 feet high, and the diameter of the pile's base is 34 feet. Thus we can form a right triangle with one leg measuring 11, the and the 
other measuring $\frac{34}{2}=17$. Also, the angle of repose $\theta$ is adjacent to the leg which is 11 feet. Thus $\tan\theta=\frac{34}{11}$ meaning
\[ \theta=\tan^{-1}\frac{34}{11}\approx 72.07 \] In the second case, we know that the angle of repose is about $72.07$, and the height of the pile is 15 feet. Let 
$d$ be the diameter of the base. Thus
\[ \tan 72.07=\frac{d}{15} \]
\[ d=15\tan 72.07 \]
\[ d\approx 46.36 \]

\section*{38}
(a): We are given that we are in 4 feet of water, $\theta_1=70^\circ$, and that $\frac{\theta_1}{\theta_2}=1.333$. Thus
\[ \frac{70}{\theta_2}=1.333 \]
\[ \theta_2=\frac{70}{1.333}\approx 52.51 \]


(b): Because $\theta_2=52.51$, 
\[ \tan 52.51=\frac{x}{4} \]
\[ x=4\tan 52.51 \]
\[ x\approx 5.21 \] feet.
Using the straight angles in the diagram, it is easy to show that the right triangle in the diagram whose legs are $4$ and $y$ is such that the angle adjacent 
to the leg measuring 4 feet measures $\theta_1$ degrees. Thus we know that
\[ \tan 70=\frac{y}{4} \]
\[ y=4\tan 70\]
\[ y\approx 10.99 \] feet.


(c): Thus $d=y-x=10.99-5.21=5.78$ feet.


(d): As we move closer, we know that the line perpendicular to the surface of the water which passes through the line from you to the goggles gets increasingly closer to 
to the goggles. Thus, as we move closer, $x$ decreases, meaning $d$ increases. 

\section*{39}
We are given that you are 100 feet from the striaghtaway, and that the cyclist is traveling 30 miles per hour. We first convert 30 miles per hour to feet per second:
\[ \frac{30miles}{1hour}=\frac{30miles}{1hour}\times\frac{1hour}{3600seconds}=\frac{1mile}{120seconds} \]
\[ =\frac{1mile}{120seconds}\times\frac{5280feet}{1mile}=\frac{44feet}{1second} \] Thus we know that the distance traveled by the cyclist $t$ seconds later is
$44t$, meaning the angle we have to turn our head to see the cyclist $t$ seconds later is
\[ \tan^{-1}(\frac{44t}{100}) \]

\section*{48}
We consider a tangent function whose graph passes through the origin and has asymptotes at $x=-\pi$ and $x=\pi$. Thus both $-\pi$ and $\pi$ are odd multiples of $\frac{\pi}{2b}$. Thus we could 
take $b=\frac{1}{2}$ or $-\frac{1}{2}$. Hence there are multiple tangent functions that satisfy the given requirenments, for example, 
$y=\tan\frac{1}{2}x$ and $y=\tan(-\frac{1}{2}x)$.

\section*{49}

\[ (\frac{3}{4}\times 2\pi,-2+2)=(\frac{3\pi}{2},0) \]
Thus we need a tangent function such that
$a\tan b(\frac{3\pi}{2}-h)+k=0$. Because $\tan^{-1}0=0$, we let $h=\frac{3\pi}{2}$, meaning the equation is $y=\tan(x-\frac{3\pi}{2})$. But this still intersects the sine graph at points other than 
the minima. An example of a function that only intersects the sine graph at the minima is $y=8\tan\frac{1}{2}\left(x-\frac{3\pi}{2}\right)$.

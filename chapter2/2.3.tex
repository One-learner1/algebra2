\section*{1}
When you add or subtract polynomials, you add or subtract the coefficients of the like terms of the polynomials.

\section*{2}
A polynomial addition problem can be solved by grouping all of the like terms of the polynomials together. Then, one can add all of the coefficients 
of these like terms together. On the other hand, if one is subtracting polynomials, the process is nearly identical to the one with addition. This time, 
however, we first need to, for whichever polynomials we are subtracting, switch the signs (multiply them by $-1$) of all the terms in this polynomial. 
We can then add all of these terms to the polynomial we were subtracting from, which has the process described above. To show that these processes are 
equivalent, we need to be able to one from the other. Well, we have shown we can do the addition process from the subtraction process. On the other hand, 
we can just multiply one of the terms in an addition of polynomials by $-1$ in order to effectively subtract it, thus proving that these two methods are 
equivalent.

\section*{56}
(For this problem, I provided the corresponding general case in each section as well, with this replacing part (d))\newline
(a): $(x^4-7x+14)+(x^2-5)=x^4+x^2-7x+9$, which has a degree of $4$\newline
Suppose the degree of $p(x)$ is $n$ and the degree of $q(x)$ is $m$. If either $n>m$ or $m>n$, the greater of the two will be the degree of 
$p(x)+q(x)$. Now consider the case where $n=m$. Consider the sequences $a_n,a_n-1,...,a_0$ and $b_m,b_m-1,...,b_0$ where every $a_i$ is the coefficient 
of the $i$th degree term in $p(x)$, and every $b_i$ is the coefficient of the $i$th degree term in $q(x)$. Because the degrees of these two polynomials 
are equal, this means that $p(x)+q(x)$ is just $(a_n+b_m)x^n+(a_n-1+b_m-1)x^{n-1}+...+(a_0+b_0)$. Whatever the first non-zero sum of $a_n$ and $b_m$'s are 
will be the degree of this new polynomial.\newline
(b): $(x^4-7x+14)-(x^2-5)=x^4-x^2-7x+19$, which has a degree of $4$\newline 
This case follows similar reasoning as the above, replacing the addition symbol with $-$, ultimately ending up with the same result.\newline
(c): $(x^4-7x+14)(x^2-5)=(x^4-7x+14)x^2-(x^4-7x+14)5=x^6-7x^3+14x^2-5x^4+35x+70=x^6-5x^4-7x^3+14x^2+35x+70$, which has a degree of $6$.\newline 
Here, the degree is $n+m$, as $a_nx^n\times b_mx^m=(a_n\times b_m)x^{n+m}$.\newline
